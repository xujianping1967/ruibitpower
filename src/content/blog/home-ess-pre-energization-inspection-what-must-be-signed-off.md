<h2 dir="auto" data-section-id="uy9wrq" data-start="66" data-end="137">The Battery Is Installed. That Does Not Mean It Is Ready to Turn On.</h2>
<p dir="auto" data-start="139" data-end="590"><strong data-start="139" data-end="590">A Home ESS should not be energized until the installer has verified the equipment against the approved design, inspected battery and inverter connections, confirmed grounding and protection, checked battery–inverter communication, reviewed isolation and backup circuits, and closed any safety-critical installation defects. The sign-off should identify who checked each item, what was measured, and whether the system is cleared for commissioning.</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="139" data-end="590"><img src="/assets/images/blog/home-ess-pre-energization-inspection-what-must-be-signed-off/1_306.jpg" alt="Home ESS Pre-Energization Inspection - What Must Be Signed Off" width="400" height="400" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="592" data-end="763">NFPA describes commissioning as the process of verifying that an ESS is installed and operating as intended before it enters service.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="765" data-end="812">For an installer, the important distinction is:</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="814" data-end="907"><strong data-start="814" data-end="907">Pre-energization inspection asks whether it is safe and correctly assembled to switch on.</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="909" data-end="972"><strong data-start="909" data-end="972">Commissioning asks whether it works correctly after you do.</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="974" data-end="1012">Those should not be the same checkbox.</p>
<h2 dir="auto" data-section-id="9zyjoz" data-start="1014" data-end="1066">1. Does the Installed Equipment Match the Design?</h2>
<p dir="auto" data-start="1068" data-end="1102">I would start with identification.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="1104" data-end="1249">The battery installed in the garage should be the battery specified in the design—not a similar model somebody substituted because stock changed.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="1251" data-end="1258">Verify:</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="1260" data-end="1294"><strong data-start="1260" data-end="1294">Battery model and module count</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="1296" data-end="1314"><strong data-start="1296" data-end="1314">Inverter model</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="1316" data-end="1353"><strong data-start="1316" data-end="1353">Battery/inverter approved pairing</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="1355" data-end="1373"><strong data-start="1355" data-end="1373">Serial numbers</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="1375" data-end="1416"><strong data-start="1375" data-end="1416">Firmware requirements where specified</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="1418" data-end="1451"><strong data-start="1418" data-end="1451">PV equipment where integrated</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="1453" data-end="1477"><strong data-start="1453" data-end="1477">Backup/EPS equipment</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="1479" data-end="1497"><strong data-start="1479" data-end="1497">Meters and CTs</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="1499" data-end="1521"><strong data-start="1499" data-end="1521">Protection devices</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="1523" data-end="1750">For a modular Ruibit/Dawnice Home ESS, module count deserves particular attention because changing the number of modules can change battery voltage, usable capacity, current capability, and the permitted inverter configuration.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="1752" data-end="1843">If equipment has been substituted, stop and verify the new combination before energization.</p>
<h2 dir="auto" data-section-id="1i756lo" data-start="1845" data-end="1896">2. Look at the Battery Before Looking at the App</h2>
<p dir="auto" data-start="1898" data-end="1949">Software cannot tell you that a cable lug is loose.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="1951" data-end="2065">The physical inspection should confirm that the battery is installed according to its model-specific instructions.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="2067" data-end="2076">Look for:</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="2078" data-end="2114"><strong data-start="2078" data-end="2114">transport or installation damage</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="2116" data-end="2139"><strong data-start="2116" data-end="2139">correct orientation</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="2141" data-end="2174"><strong data-start="2141" data-end="2174">stable floor or wall mounting</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="2176" data-end="2195"><strong data-start="2176" data-end="2195">stacking limits</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="2197" data-end="2220"><strong data-start="2197" data-end="2220">required clearances</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="2222" data-end="2247"><strong data-start="2222" data-end="2247">unblocked ventilation</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="2249" data-end="2306"><strong data-start="2249" data-end="2306">water exposure or unsuitable environmental conditions</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="2308" data-end="2334"><strong data-start="2308" data-end="2334">accessible disconnects</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="2336" data-end="2354"><strong data-start="2336" data-end="2354">service access</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="2356" data-end="2415">This is especially important with stackable home batteries.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="2417" data-end="2539">A tower may look neat while one module is not fully seated or the installer has left insufficient room to remove it later.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="2541" data-end="2742">NFPA guidance also highlights system location, separation, maintenance accessibility, electrical isolation, and physical protection as installation considerations.</p>
<h2 dir="auto" data-section-id="ocbopm" data-start="2744" data-end="2788">3. Check Polarity Before Closing Anything</h2>
<p dir="auto" data-start="2790" data-end="2837">This is the moment for a meter, not confidence.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="2839" data-end="2998">Before energization, qualified personnel should verify the electrical installation according to the manufacturer's procedure and applicable local requirements.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3000" data-end="3051">Depending on system architecture, that can include:</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3053" data-end="3068"><strong data-start="3053" data-end="3068">DC polarity</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3070" data-end="3089"><strong data-start="3070" data-end="3089">battery voltage</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3091" data-end="3105"><strong data-start="3091" data-end="3105">AC voltage</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3107" data-end="3138"><strong data-start="3107" data-end="3138">protective earth continuity</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3140" data-end="3168"><strong data-start="3140" data-end="3168">conductor identification</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3170" data-end="3194"><strong data-start="3170" data-end="3194">terminal connections</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3196" data-end="3220"><strong data-start="3196" data-end="3220">disconnect positions</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3222" data-end="3246"><strong data-start="3222" data-end="3246">breaker/fuse ratings</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3248" data-end="3274"><strong data-start="3248" data-end="3274">cable size and routing</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3276" data-end="3309"><strong data-start="3276" data-end="3309">torque records where required</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3311" data-end="3400">A reversed battery connection is not something the commissioning process should discover.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3402" data-end="3467">Neither is an incorrectly landed neutral or protective conductor.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3469" data-end="3651">For high-voltage Home ESS, the manufacturer's prescribed sequence is particularly important because battery strings can contain hazardous DC voltage before the inverter is operating.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3469" data-end="3651"><img src="/assets/images/blog/home-ess-pre-energization-inspection-what-must-be-signed-off/1_832.jpg" alt="Home ESS Pre-Energization Inspection - What Must Be Signed Off" width="400" height="400" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></p>
<h2 dir="auto" data-section-id="1y24n3e" data-start="3653" data-end="3708">4. The BMS and Inverter Need to Recognize Each Other</h2>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3710" data-end="3755">Correct voltage does not prove communication.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3757" data-end="3845">Before normal operation, verify the specified communication interface and configuration:</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3847" data-end="3874"><strong data-start="3847" data-end="3874">CAN or RS485 connection</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3876" data-end="3906"><strong data-start="3876" data-end="3906">correct communication port</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3908" data-end="3938"><strong data-start="3908" data-end="3938">termination where required</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3940" data-end="3972"><strong data-start="3940" data-end="3972">battery address/DIP settings</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="3974" data-end="4004"><strong data-start="3974" data-end="4004">master/slave configuration</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4006" data-end="4039"><strong data-start="4006" data-end="4039">inverter battery type/profile</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4041" data-end="4063"><strong data-start="4041" data-end="4063">supported firmware</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4065" data-end="4198">Once control power is available under the manufacturer's startup procedure, the inverter should report plausible battery information.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4200" data-end="4212">For example:</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4214" data-end="4221"><strong data-start="4214" data-end="4221">SoC</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4223" data-end="4242"><strong data-start="4223" data-end="4242">battery voltage</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4244" data-end="4259"><strong data-start="4244" data-end="4259">temperature</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4261" data-end="4288"><strong data-start="4261" data-end="4288">charge/discharge limits</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4290" data-end="4306"><strong data-start="4290" data-end="4306">alarm status</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4308" data-end="4414">If the inverter shows 50% SoC while the BMS reports 78%, do not proceed because "the battery still works."</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4416" data-end="4442">Resolve the data mismatch.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4444" data-end="4552">A Home ESS depends on the BMS communicating operating limits to the inverter, not merely exchanging packets.</p>
<h2 dir="auto" data-section-id="1y6tiks" data-start="4554" data-end="4623">5. Check the CT Direction Before It Creates a Very Strange Battery</h2>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4625" data-end="4675">The CT or smart meter deserves its own inspection.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4677" data-end="4737">Suppose the system is configured for solar self-consumption.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4739" data-end="4762">The house imports 2 kW.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4764" data-end="4787">But the CT is reversed.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4789" data-end="4882">The inverter may interpret that import as export and make exactly the wrong control decision.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4884" data-end="4914">Before commissioning, confirm:</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4916" data-end="4934"><strong data-start="4916" data-end="4934">CT orientation</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4936" data-end="4956"><strong data-start="4936" data-end="4956">phase assignment</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4958" data-end="4974"><strong data-start="4958" data-end="4974">meter wiring</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4976" data-end="4993"><strong data-start="4976" data-end="4993">communication</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="4995" data-end="5049"><strong data-start="4995" data-end="5049">location relative to loads, PV and grid connection</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="5051" data-end="5124">For three-phase installations, phase mapping becomes even more important.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="5126" data-end="5232">The monitoring screen should eventually agree with an independent understanding of actual site power flow.</p>
<h2 dir="auto" data-section-id="nf434c" data-start="5234" data-end="5281">6. Backup Circuits Need a Different Sign-Off</h2>
<p dir="auto" data-start="5283" data-end="5384">If the homeowner purchased backup, I want the backup boundary clearly identified before energization.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="5386" data-end="5415">Which circuits are protected?</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="5417" data-end="5450">Which are intentionally excluded?</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="5452" data-end="5517">Does the EPS/backup output feed a dedicated essential-load panel?</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="5519" data-end="5582">Could the backup source unintentionally energize the grid side?</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="5584" data-end="5685">Are high-power loads such as EV charging or resistance heating excluded where required by the design?</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="5687" data-end="5753">This is also the point to check labels and isolation arrangements.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="5755" data-end="5969">DOE notes that battery-storage installations may introduce additional electrical and fire-code requirements and recommends involving utilities and applicable authorities early.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="5971" data-end="6026">Actual backup operation is tested during commissioning.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="6028" data-end="6104">But the wiring that makes that test safe should already have been inspected.</p>
<h2 dir="auto" data-section-id="hbgvse" data-start="6106" data-end="6169">7. Do Not Sign Off an Installation With an Unexplained Alarm</h2>
<p dir="auto" data-start="6171" data-end="6290">Once the permitted auxiliary/control systems are powered during the prescribed startup sequence, review initial status.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="6292" data-end="6307">I would expect:</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="6309" data-end="6337"><strong data-start="6309" data-end="6337">no unexplained BMS fault</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="6339" data-end="6373"><strong data-start="6339" data-end="6373">no insulation/protection fault</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="6375" data-end="6405"><strong data-start="6375" data-end="6405">plausible cell/module data</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="6407" data-end="6438"><strong data-start="6407" data-end="6438">normal temperature readings</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="6440" data-end="6475"><strong data-start="6440" data-end="6475">inverter recognizes the battery</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="6477" data-end="6513"><strong data-start="6477" data-end="6513">meter/CT communication available</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="6515" data-end="6567"><strong data-start="6515" data-end="6567">monitoring connection established where required</strong></p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="6569" data-end="6648">An alarm that "will probably disappear after commissioning" is not a diagnosis.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="6650" data-end="6675">Record it and resolve it.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="6677" data-end="6888">DOE's energy-storage safety strategy emphasizes that acceptance testing establishes a baseline for expected BESS behavior and that results should be catalogued and archived.</p>
<p dir="auto" data-start="6890" data-end="6975">The same documentation discipline should begin before the first full operating cycle.</p>

<h3>FAQs</h3>
<p><strong>Q: Is CE marking mandatory for home batteries sold in the EU?</strong> Yes. Any battery placed on the EU market must comply with the Low Voltage Directive, EMC Directive, and the new Battery Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, and carry the CE mark.</p>
<p><strong>Q: What is the most important CE document to request?</strong> The Declaration of Conformity (DoC). It is the signed document that lists all applicable directives, the exact product model, and the EU importer. Without it, the CE logo on the box is meaningless.</p>
<p><strong>Q: Is UN 38.3 the same as CE?</strong> No. UN 38.3 proves the battery is safe to transport by air, sea, and road. It does not prove the battery meets EU market safety rules. You need both.</p>
<p><strong>Q: Can I check the CE certificate myself?</strong> Yes. Ask the supplier for the notified body number and test report number, then verify them on the EU NANDO database and by emailing the issuing laboratory directly.</p>
<p><strong>Q: What happens if my supplier cannot provide CE documents?</strong> Do not order. Shipments without proper CE paperwork are commonly held at EU customs for weeks, and installers cannot legally commission the system without them.</p>
