Energy Storage Guide

How Does a Commercial Battery Energy Storage System Work?

A commercial BESS charges when power is available or inexpensive, stores energy in batteries, then discharges when the site needs backup, peak shaving or solar shifting.

Commercial energy storage is best understood as a controlled power system, not just a battery cabinet. The battery stores DC energy, the PCS converts power between AC and DC, and the EMS decides when the system should charge, discharge or standby.

Main system blocks

  • Battery modules or racks: store energy using LiFePO4 cells.
  • BMS: monitors voltage, current, temperature and protection status.
  • PCS: converts power between the grid/load side and the battery side.
  • EMS: controls operating strategy, schedules and energy flow.
  • Thermal and fire systems: help keep the system within safe operating limits.

Operating modes

In peak shaving, the BESS discharges during high-demand periods. In solar storage, it stores excess PV generation and releases it later. In backup mode, it supports critical loads when grid power fails. A project can combine several modes, but the control logic should be defined before equipment selection.

What buyers should confirm

Ask for rated power, usable capacity, round-trip efficiency boundary, operating temperature, communication protocol, protection design and whether the PCS and EMS are included in the scope.

Selection tip: a battery with enough kWh may still be unsuitable if PCS power, switching logic or cooling capacity does not match the duty cycle.
