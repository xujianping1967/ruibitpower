1-Hour, 2-Hour or 4-Hour BESS: How to Choose Duration

Storage duration connects power and energy. A 500kW / 1MWh system is roughly a 2-hour BESS before real-world efficiency and reserve settings.

Duration should follow the business case. A project designed for short peak shaving does not need the same battery duration as a solar shifting or backup project.

Common duration choices

  • 1-hour: short peaks, fast support and limited backup windows.
  • 2-hour: common balance for C&I peak shaving and solar self-consumption.
  • 4-hour: longer solar shifting, extended backup or grid-service projects.

What changes with longer duration

More duration usually means more battery capacity relative to PCS power. That may improve runtime but also increases battery cost, footprint and project economics sensitivity.

How to choose

Map the load curve first. Identify when peaks happen, how long they last, how much solar is available and what backup runtime is required. Then match kW and kWh together.

Selection tip: do not choose a 4-hour system just because it sounds safer. Choose it because the load curve or revenue model requires it.
