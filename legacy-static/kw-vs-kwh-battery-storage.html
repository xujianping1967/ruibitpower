kW vs kWh in Battery Storage

kW describes how much power a system can deliver at one moment. kWh describes how much energy it can store or deliver over time.

Many storage sizing mistakes happen because buyers compare only kWh. A battery may have enough stored energy but still fail to support a high peak load if inverter or PCS power is too small.

Simple difference

kW is power. It answers: how much load can the system support right now? kWh is energy. It answers: how long can the system support that load?

Example

A 100kWh battery supporting a 50kW load may run for roughly two hours before considering usable depth, efficiency and reserve settings. The same 100kWh battery supporting a 100kW load may run for roughly one hour.

Why it matters in C&I BESS

Peak shaving often needs enough kW to reduce site demand. Backup power needs enough kWh to cover runtime. EV charging storage may need both high kW and enough kWh to cover charging peaks.

Ruibit Power recommendation: provide peak load and backup duration together. Capacity without power demand is not enough for accurate configuration.
